Your toolkit is built around your barriers -- no two toolkits should look the same.

A toolkit for someone with chronic fatigue looks different from one for someone with visual impairment, which looks different from one for someone with ADHD. The best toolkit is the one built around your specific barriers.

Start by listing the tasks that cost you the most energy, cause the most pain, or get dropped most often. Those are your priorities. Everything else is secondary.

Essentials

The core toolkit every disabled person should consider.

AI
Claude or ChatGPT (your AI brain)
The core tool for drafting, summarizing, planning, researching, and problem-solving. Claude has a generous free tier and excels at nuanced writing. This is your daily workhorse.
VOICE
Built-in voice control (your hands-free interface)
Every device has free voice dictation and voice commands. Set up once, use forever. Essential for anyone with hand/arm pain, fatigue, or mobility limitations.
CAL
Calendar app with reminders (your memory)
Google Calendar, Apple Calendar — free and reliable. Put everything in the calendar with reminders. If it is not in the calendar, it does not exist. Your external memory system.
NOTES
Notes app (your filing cabinet)
Apple Notes, Google Keep, or Notion. Store your personal info template, medication list, doctor contacts, and AI prompt templates here. One place for everything you need to reference.
Workflows

Build workflows for your hardest tasks.

Custom Workflow Examples

Morning Routine Workflow For people who cannot face decisions in the morning.
1. Calendar sends daily overview at 8am 2. Pre-written note tells you: take meds, eat something, check today's one priority 3. If you need to communicate today, Claude drafts messages while you eat 4. All decisions were made yesterday. Morning-you just follows the list. Decision-free mornings = preserved energy.
Medical Appointment Workflow For people who forget to prep and forget what happened.
1. Calendar reminder 3 days before: "Send symptom update to Claude" 2. Claude creates appointment summary 3. Print or save to phone 4. At appointment: read from summary 5. After: dictate what the doctor said into Claude 6. Claude creates post-visit summary and action items 7. Add follow-up tasks to calendar Nothing falls through the cracks.
Bill Day Workflow For people who avoid finances until it is a crisis.
1. Calendar reminder on the 1st: "10-minute money check" 2. Open banking app, note balance 3. Paste to Claude with bill list 4. Claude flags any problems 5. Handle problems or confirm all clear 6. Close banking app. Done for the month. The whole thing takes 10 minutes.
Crisis Communication Workflow For bad episodes when you need to cancel everything.
1. Pre-written text templates saved in Notes: - Cancel appointment - Out sick from work - Need extension on deadline - Cannot make social plans 2. During crisis: open Notes, copy template, send 3. No drafting, no explaining, no energy spent Build the crisis kit during good days.
Sovereignty

Own your tools. Do not rent your independence.

Here is a hard truth: if your entire system depends on one company's product, and that company changes its pricing, terms, or shuts down — you lose your infrastructure. For disabled people, losing infrastructure is not just inconvenient. It can be dangerous.

The sovereign approach: Use multiple tools so no single point of failure exists. Keep your personal data (info template, med list, contacts) in local files you control, not just in cloud apps. Export your data regularly. Have a backup for every critical system. Your independence should not depend on any single company's business decisions.

Practical sovereignty checklist

Can you switch AI providers and still function? Is your personal info saved locally, not just in the cloud? Do you have backup reminders if your phone dies? Can someone else access your critical info in an emergency? If any answer is no, that is your next thing to fix.

The key insight: Your toolkit should be built around your barriers, not around what is trendy. Choose tools that address your specific challenges. Build workflows that run even on your worst days. Own your infrastructure. Your independence depends on it.

Key concepts to remember.

Building Your AI Toolkit

What are the four core toolkit components?
1. AI assistant (Claude/ChatGPT) as your brain. 2. Voice control as your hands-free interface. 3. Calendar with reminders as your memory. 4. Notes app as your filing cabinet.
How do you choose which tools to prioritize?
List the tasks that cost you the most energy, cause the most pain, or get dropped most often. Solve those first. Everything else is secondary.
What is a crisis communication workflow?
Pre-written text templates (cancel appointment, out sick, need extension) saved in Notes. During a crisis, copy and send. No drafting, no explaining, no energy spent.
What is tool sovereignty?
Using multiple tools so no single point of failure exists. Keeping data locally. Having backups for critical systems. Your independence should not depend on any single company.
What makes a good morning routine for executive dysfunction?
No decisions. Calendar sends the overview. Pre-written notes tell you what to do. All decisions were made the day before. Morning-you just follows the list.
Should you build workflows during good or bad periods?
Always during good periods. Build the systems when you have energy so they run when you do not. The crisis kit gets built before the crisis.
