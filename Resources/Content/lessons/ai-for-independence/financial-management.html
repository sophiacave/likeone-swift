Financial management is not about discipline -- it is about systems that run when you cannot.

Disabled people face higher costs (medications, equipment, transportation, copays) and often lower income (reduced work hours, gaps in employment, discrimination). This financial pressure means every dollar matters — but executive dysfunction, brain fog, and overwhelm make money management feel impossible.

The result: late fees on bills you forgot. Overdraft charges because you lost track. Medical debt because you did not appeal an insurance denial in time. Not because you are irresponsible. Because the system demands executive function you do not always have.

AI cannot make you richer. But it can make sure you never miss a bill, never pay a fee you could avoid, and always know where you stand financially — with minimal energy spent.

Bills

Never miss a bill again.

Bill tracker setup prompt
Create a monthly bill tracker for me. Here are my
recurring bills:

[List your bills: name, amount, due date]
Example:
- Rent: $1,200, due 1st
- Electric: ~$80, due 15th
- Phone: $45, due 20th
- Insurance: $350, due 1st

For each bill:
1. Which ones can be set to autopay?
2. Which ones need manual payment?
3. Create a calendar reminder schedule (3 days before
   each due date)
4. Calculate my total monthly fixed costs
5. Flag which bills have late fees and how much
Autopay everything possible

The single best financial move for people with executive dysfunction: set every bill to autopay. Ask AI: "Which of my bills can I set to autopay, and what are the steps for each one?" Autopay means the bill gets paid even when you cannot function. It is insurance against bad episodes.

Budgeting

A budget that accounts for disability costs.

Standard budget advice ignores disability costs. "Just cut the subscriptions!" does not work when your "subscriptions" include medication delivery services, ride-share because you cannot drive, and meal delivery because you cannot cook during flare-ups.

Disability-Aware Budgeting

What disability costs should a budget include?
Medications and copays. Medical equipment and supplies. Accessible transportation (Uber/Lyft when you cannot drive). Meal delivery during bad periods. Therapy and mental health. Assistive technology. Home modifications. Higher utility bills (temperature regulation). These are not luxuries — they are necessities.
How to create a disability-aware budget with AI
PROMPT: "Create a monthly budget. My income is [amount]. My disability-related costs include: [list them]. My regular bills are: [list them]. After fixed costs, how much is left for variable expenses? What is a realistic grocery and household budget?" AI calculates without judgment.
The emergency buffer
AI can help you build a small emergency buffer: "I have $[amount] left after bills. Help me create a plan to save $500 for emergencies over [realistic timeframe]. Account for the fact that some months I will have unexpected medical costs." Even $50/month adds up.
Finding money you are leaving on the table
PROMPT: "Review my spending and identify: 1) Subscriptions I might not be using 2) Bills where I might qualify for a lower rate or assistance program 3) Disability-specific tax deductions I might be missing 4) Benefits or programs I am not accessing" AI finds money most people miss.
Decoding

Understand what you are actually being charged.

Medical bills, insurance Explanation of Benefits, utility bills with mysterious charges — paste them into AI and ask for a plain English translation.

Bill decoder prompt
Explain this bill/statement in plain English:

[paste the bill details]

Tell me:
1. What am I actually being charged for?
2. Does this amount seem right?
3. Is anything on here that I should question or
   dispute?
4. Is there a payment plan option I should ask about?
The System

A 10-minute monthly money check-in.

1
Check bank balance (1 min)
Open your banking app. Note the balance. That is the starting point.
2
List upcoming bills (2 min)
Ask AI: "My balance is $X. My upcoming bills this month are [list]. Will I cover everything? Flag any problems."
3
Handle any problems (5 min)
If something is tight, ask AI: "I am short $X this month. What are my options? Are any bills flexible? Can I call anyone for an extension?"
4
Done (2 min)
Set a calendar reminder for the same check-in next month. Close the banking app. You are done for 30 days.

The key insight: Financial management for disabled people is not about discipline — it is about systems. Autopay handles the bills. AI handles the math. A 10-minute monthly check-in handles the awareness. Build the system once. It runs even when you cannot.

Review

Key concepts to remember.

Financial Management

What is the autopay rule?
Set every possible bill to autopay. Bills get paid even during bad episodes. Executive dysfunction cannot cause late fees if the payment is automatic.
What disability costs do standard budgets miss?
Medications, copays, accessible transportation, meal delivery, therapy, assistive tech, home modifications, higher utilities. These are necessities, not luxuries.
How long should a monthly financial check-in take?
10 minutes. Check balance, list upcoming bills, handle any problems, set next reminder. Done for 30 days.
How do you decode a confusing bill?
Paste it into AI and ask: What am I being charged for? Does this seem right? Should I dispute anything? Is there a payment plan option?
What if you are short on money this month?
Ask AI: "I am short $X. What are my options?" AI can identify flexible bills, help draft extension requests, and find assistance programs.
How do you find money you are leaving on the table?
Ask AI to review for: unused subscriptions, lower-rate options, disability tax deductions, and benefits programs you are not accessing.
