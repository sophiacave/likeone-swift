Master the healthcare system was not built for disabled people. step by step.

Short appointments where you forget to mention important symptoms. Insurance denials written in legal language. Medical records you cannot understand. Phone trees that take 40 minutes to navigate. The system requires advocacy skills and energy at the exact moments when you have the least of both.

AI does not replace a good doctor or a good therapist. But it gives you the preparation, documentation, and language to get the most out of every medical interaction — even when your brain is not cooperating.

Appointments

Walk into every appointment prepared.

The number one regret after a doctor appointment: "I forgot to mention..." AI prevents this.

Appointment prep prompt
I have a [type] appointment with Dr. [name] on
[date]. Help me prepare:

My conditions: [list]
Current medications: [list]
New symptoms since last visit: [describe]
Questions I want to ask: [list any you have]

Create:
1. A one-page summary I can hand the doctor
2. A list of questions I should ask (including any I
   might not think of)
3. A list of symptoms organized by severity
4. Reminder: what to bring (insurance card, med list,
   etc.)

Keep the summary concise — doctors have limited time.
Print it or keep it on your phone

Print the AI-generated summary or keep it open on your phone. When brain fog hits mid-appointment, you read from the list instead of trying to remember. This one strategy changes the quality of every medical visit.

Records

Translate doctor-speak into plain English.

Medical Record Translation

Lab Results Your blood work came back with numbers and ranges that mean nothing to you.
PROMPT: "Explain these lab results in plain English. What is normal, what is not? Which results should I ask my doctor about? What do the abnormal ones potentially mean?" AI translates every value and flags what matters.
Doctor Notes Your medical records have notes from your doctor with abbreviations and medical terms.
PROMPT: "Translate these doctor notes into plain English. What did the doctor observe? What is their assessment? What is the plan? Explain any medical terms." You have a right to understand your own records.
Medication Information You were prescribed something new and want to understand it before taking it.
PROMPT: "Explain this medication in plain English: [medication name, dose]. What does it do? Common side effects? Interactions with [your other medications]? What should I watch for? What should I tell my doctor about?" Note: always confirm with your pharmacist too.
Imaging Reports MRI, X-ray, or CT results with medical terminology.
PROMPT: "Explain this imaging report in plain English. What did they find? Is anything abnormal? What does [specific term] mean? What are the next steps based on these findings?" AI makes radiology reports readable.
Insurance Fights

Fight insurance denials without burning out.

Insurance companies deny claims expecting most people to give up. Do not give up. Let AI fight for you.

Insurance appeal prompt
My insurance denied [treatment/medication/procedure].
Their reason: [paste the denial reason].
My condition: [your diagnosis].
My doctor recommended this because: [reason].

Write a formal appeal letter that:
1. References the specific denial and claim number
2. Explains medical necessity
3. Cites relevant medical guidelines if applicable
4. Requests a peer-to-peer review
5. Notes the appeal deadline
6. Is professional but firm

Address it to [insurance company appeals department].

Important: AI-drafted appeal letters are a starting point. Have your doctor review and sign off on any medical claims in the letter. The combination of AI-drafted language and your doctor's authority is powerful.

Research

Research without falling into misinformation.

The research prompt that avoids misinformation

"Explain [condition/treatment] based on current medical consensus. Cite major medical organizations (NIH, WHO, Mayo Clinic). What does the evidence actually show? What is still uncertain? What questions should I ask my doctor?" AI sticks to evidence-based information when you tell it to.

Preparing questions for your doctor

"I have [condition]. I read about [treatment/approach]. Help me create a list of informed questions to ask my doctor about whether this could be appropriate for me. I want to have a productive conversation, not demand a specific treatment."

The key insight: You deserve to understand your own health, fight for your own coverage, and walk into every appointment prepared. AI gives you the tools to be your own best advocate — even when your brain, body, and energy are working against you.

Review

Key concepts to remember.

Medical Advocacy

What should an appointment prep include?
One-page summary for the doctor, list of questions, symptoms organized by severity, and reminders of what to bring. Print it or keep it on your phone.
How do you understand medical records?
Paste them into AI and ask for a plain English translation. AI explains lab results, doctor notes, medication info, and imaging reports in language you can understand.
How do you fight an insurance denial?
Use AI to draft a formal appeal letter citing medical necessity and requesting peer-to-peer review. Have your doctor review and sign off. File before the deadline.
How do you research a condition without misinformation?
Ask AI to explain based on "current medical consensus" and cite major medical organizations. Ask what the evidence shows and what questions to bring to your doctor.
Why is appointment prep so important for disabled people?
Short appointments, brain fog, and anxiety mean you forget to mention symptoms and ask questions. A prepared summary fixes this.
Should you trust AI medical information completely?
No. AI provides excellent summaries and preparation, but always confirm with your actual doctors and pharmacists. AI is a tool for informed advocacy, not a replacement for medical advice.
