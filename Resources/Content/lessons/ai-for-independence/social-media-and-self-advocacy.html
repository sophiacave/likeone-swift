Why This Matters

Your story has power. But telling it should not drain you.

Disabled people are underrepresented online. When you share your experience, you do two things: you help other disabled people feel less alone, and you educate non-disabled people about realities they do not see. That matters.

But content creation takes energy. Writing a post, editing it, finding the right tone, worrying about responses — it is exhausting. For people with limited spoons, the cost of advocacy can be too high.

AI changes the equation. It drafts the post, refines the tone, handles the structure. You provide the experience and the truth. The energy cost drops from an hour to minutes.

Creating Content

Turn your thoughts into polished posts in 2 minutes.

Social Media Prompts for Advocacy

Sharing an experience Something happened that highlights disability life and you want to share it.
PROMPT: "I want to write a social media post about [experience]. Here is what happened: [your raw, unfiltered version]. Turn this into a compelling post for [Twitter/Instagram/LinkedIn]. Keep my voice authentic. Make it impactful but not pity-seeking. Under 280 characters for Twitter or 3 short paragraphs for Instagram." AI polishes without sanitizing.
Educational content You want to teach people something about your disability or disability in general.
PROMPT: "Write an educational social media post explaining [topic — e.g., what spoon theory is, why accessible design matters, what executive dysfunction feels like]. Audience: non-disabled people who genuinely want to learn. Tone: informative and warm, not angry or lecture-y. Include a concrete example." Education without exhaustion.
Responding to ignorance Someone said something ableist and you want to respond thoughtfully.
PROMPT: "Someone said [what they said]. Write a response that educates without being hostile. Explain why this is problematic, offer a better perspective, and keep it brief. I want to change minds, not start fights." AI helps you stay composed when emotions run high.
Celebrating a win You accomplished something that feels small to able-bodied people but is huge for you.
PROMPT: "I want to share an accomplishment: [what you did]. For context, this is significant because [why it is hard for you]. Write a post that celebrates this without being self-deprecating. Tone: proud and genuine. Help people understand why this matters." Your wins deserve visibility too.
The System

Create a week of content in one good hour.

The smartest approach for variable-energy creators: batch your content during good periods. Create a week or month of posts when you have the energy, then schedule them to post automatically.

Batch content prompt
Create 7 social media posts for me to schedule
throughout next week. Mix of:

- 2 personal experience posts about living with
  [your condition]
- 2 educational posts about [disability topic]
- 1 resource share (recommend a tool, app, or
  community for disabled people)
- 1 light/funny post about disability life
- 1 engagement post (ask a question)

Platform: [Twitter/Instagram/LinkedIn]
My voice: [describe your tone — direct, warm, witty,
serious, etc.]
Topics I care about: [list 3-5]
Schedule tools

Use Buffer, Later, or the built-in scheduling in Twitter/Instagram to schedule posts ahead of time. Create during good days. Your online presence stays consistent even during bad periods.

Protection

Protecting yourself while being visible.

You do not owe anyone your full story

Share what you want to share. You can educate about disability without disclosing your specific diagnosis, medications, or worst moments. Ask AI: "Help me share [this experience] without disclosing [specific details I want to keep private]."

Pre-written responses for trolls

Ask AI to draft responses to common ableist comments. Save them as quick-reply templates. When someone is ignorant, paste the response instead of drafting one from scratch. Protect your energy.

The mute/block strategy

You are not obligated to engage with everyone. Mute and block liberally. Your mental health is more important than educating every stranger. Save your advocacy energy for people who are genuinely willing to learn.

The key insight: Your voice matters in the disability conversation. AI makes that voice sustainable. Create when you can, schedule for when you cannot. Share on your terms, protect your boundaries, and let AI handle the production so you can focus on the truth.
