Practice

Which template fits the job?

Match the Template

1Your quarterly numbers are in and your manager needs a summary by end of day. Which template?

Explanation: The Report Writer template gives you executive summary, metrics table, wins, challenges, and next steps — exactly what a quarterly summary needs.

2A frustrated customer emailed about a billing error on their account. Which template?

Explanation: The Customer Response template is built for exactly this: it includes fields for the customer message, resolution, tone, and the critical instruction to acknowledge frustration before offering solutions.

3You need to decide between two vendors for your company CRM. Which template?

Explanation: The Decision Memo template structures a clear recommendation with a comparison table, criteria weighting, and risk assessment — exactly what you need to present a vendor decision.
Your Assignment

Build your own template library.

The templates above cover common tasks, but your most powerful templates will be the ones you build for YOUR recurring work.

Prompt Templates for Work

1What is the main advantage of using prompt templates instead of writing prompts from scratch?

Explanation: Templates lock in proven patterns. The structure, voice, and quality standards stay consistent while only the variables change between uses.

2Which template would you use if your boss asks you to prepare talking points for tomorrow's board meeting?

Explanation: The Meeting Prep template generates an agenda, talking points, likely questions with responses, and a preparation question — exactly what you need before a high-stakes meeting.

3What makes the Analyst template different from the Content Creator template?

Explanation: The Analyst template is designed for examining existing data to surface insights, while the Content Creator template generates new material. Different goals require different prompt structures.

4You write the same type of weekly report every Friday. What should you do?

Explanation: Any recurring task should be templatized. Take your best prompt, replace the changing parts with variable slots, and save it. Within a month you will have a personal prompt library covering 80% of your AI work.

Within a month, you'll have a personal prompt library that handles 80% of your AI work in seconds.

How to build your first template:

  1. Identify a task you do at least once a week — weekly reports, client emails, meeting prep, content creation
  2. Write the best prompt you can for it — use the full RCFCE framework, spend extra time getting it right
  3. Run it and iterate — refine until the output is exactly what you want
  4. Replace the specific details with variable slots — [client name], [this week's metrics], [meeting topic]
  5. Save it somewhere accessible — a note, a doc, a text expander, wherever you can grab it in seconds
  6. Use and improve — every time you use the template, notice what you tweak and update the template accordingly

The templates in this lesson are your starting library. But the ones you build yourself — tuned to YOUR voice, YOUR work, YOUR audience — will always outperform generic ones. Start with one template this week. Add one more next week. By month's end, you'll wonder how you ever worked without them.
