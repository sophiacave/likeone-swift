RCFCE Framework Review

R — Role
Who should the AI be? Sets expertise, perspective, and voice. Example: You are a senior copywriter specializing in SaaS landing pages.
C — Context
The situation, background, audience, and why this matters. The most impactful type of context is usually the audience.
F — Format
What the output should look like — bullets, table, email, blog post, JSON. Prevents the AI from defaulting to generic paragraphs.
C — Constraints
Boundaries like word count, tone, things to avoid. Negative constraints (what NOT to do) are surprisingly effective at eliminating AI-isms.
E — Examples
Samples that show the AI what good looks like. The most underused component — even 2-3 examples transform output quality.

RCFCE Framework — 5 Questions

1What does RCFCE stand for?

Explanation: Role, Context, Format, Constraints, Examples — the five building blocks of every effective prompt.

2Which two RCFCE components are the bare minimum for any effective prompt?

Explanation: Context (what you need) and Format (what the output should look like) are the essentials. These two alone put you ahead of 90% of AI users.

3When is the best time to add a Role to your prompt?

Explanation: Roles shine when you need depth, expertise, or a specific perspective. Skip them for quick factual questions or simple transformations.

4What is the most impactful type of context for most business prompts?

Explanation: Audience is the single most impactful context type. It changes vocabulary, tone, depth, and structure more than any other piece of information.

5Why are negative constraints (what NOT to do) often more effective than positive ones?

Explanation: Negative constraints directly target the specific AI habits you hate — buzzwords, preambles, hedging, sycophantic openers. Banning them forces more authentic, natural output.

Diagnose and fix.

What Is Wrong With This Prompt? — 5 Questions

1"Write something about social media marketing for my business." — What is the PRIMARY issue?

Explanation: Something about is the red flag. No specific format, no audience, no business details, no tone. The AI has to guess everything and will produce generic output.

2"You are an experienced financial advisor. My client just inherited $200K and has $40K in student loans. They are risk-averse and want to retire by 55." — What is missing?

Explanation: There is no Format specified. What output does the user want? A step-by-step plan? Bullet points? A comparison table? Without format, AI defaults to generic paragraphs.

3"Write a 500-word blog post about remote work trends. Use bullet points for the main trends. Casual, conversational tone." — What would improve this most?

Explanation: Who is writing this — a recruiter, CEO, freelancer? And who reads it — HR professionals, remote workers, managers? The perspective and audience change everything about the content.

4AI gave you a blog post full of phrases like "in today's fast-paced digital landscape" and "leverage synergies." What is the fastest fix?

Explanation: A targeted negative constraint follow-up is the fastest fix. The content structure is probably fine — you just need to kill the AI-isms.

5You have iterated 4 times and the output is still not right. What should you do?

Explanation: After 3+ rounds with no improvement, the original prompt has a fundamental issue. Start fresh, but use what you learned from the failed attempts to write a better prompt.
Part 3

Match technique to situation.

Which Technique Fits? — 5 Questions

1You got a decent blog post from AI, but it sounds too corporate. Which technique should you use?

Explanation: Tone Shift is the right move. The content is fine — you just need a different voice. Try: Rewrite this in a conversational tone, like explaining to a friend over coffee.

2AI gave you an overview of 5 marketing strategies, but you need deep detail on strategy #3 specifically. Which technique?

Explanation: Zoom In keeps the good foundation and drills deeper: Expand on strategy #3. Give me specific steps, timeline, and expected results for a team of 5.

3You asked for a sales email but AI wrote an informational article instead. Which technique?

Explanation: The AI went in the wrong direction entirely. Redirect corrects course: This should be a direct sales email, not an article. Rewrite as an email with a subject line, one key benefit, and a CTA.

4You have a good headline but want to see alternative options. Which technique?

Explanation: Remix generates variations: Give me 5 completely different versions of this headline. Make each one take a different angle — curiosity, urgency, social proof, humor, and simplicity.

5AI gave you a good answer but used too many buzzwords. What is the fastest fix?

Explanation: A targeted negative constraint follow-up is fastest. The structure and content are good — you just need to kill the AI-isms in one pass.

Lesson 6 — The Art of Constraints: 5 constraint types: length, tone, scope, audience, negative. Negative constraints are the secret weapon — they directly eliminate AI-isms. The "invisible guardrail" pairs positives with negatives for precise tone.
Lesson 7 — Iteration and Refinement: 4 follow-up patterns: Zoom In, Tone Shift, Redirect, Remix. The 3-step refinement chain (draft, critique, polish). The "best of both" technique. Start fresh after 3 failed rounds.
Lesson 8 — Templates for Work: 9 ready-to-use templates covering emails, content, analysis, meetings, brainstorming, editing, reports, decisions, and customer responses. Build your own template library for recurring tasks.

Part 4

Put it all together.

Putting It All Together — 5 Questions

1You need to write a weekly team update email. What is the most efficient approach?

Explanation: A template locks in the structure, tone, and format that works — you only swap out the weekly data. This is the template library approach from Lesson 8.

2Your prompt has Context, Format, and Constraints but the output feels generic. What should you add?

Explanation: A Role is the most likely missing piece. You are a senior product strategist produces dramatically different output than no role. The expertise filter changes vocabulary, depth, and specificity.

3You are building a prompt for a complex task (board presentation). Which RCFCE components should you include?

Explanation: High-stakes, complex tasks benefit from all five components. The Role sets expertise. Context provides the situation. Format structures the output. Constraints eliminate generic filler. Examples show what good looks like.

4AI gave you two responses to the same prompt. Response 1 has a great opening, Response 2 has better structure. What do you do?

Explanation: The best of both technique from Lesson 7. AI excels at synthesis — tell it exactly which elements to take from each version and it will produce something better than either alone.

5What is the SINGLE most important habit for long-term prompt writing improvement?

Explanation: Iteration is the master skill. Perfect prompts on the first try are rare even for experts. The ability to diagnose what is wrong and fix it in a follow-up is what separates good from great AI users.

Prompt Writing Final Challenge

1What does the R in RCFCE stand for?

Advanced Diagnostic — 5 Questions

1A coworker asks AI: "Tell me about project management." They get a generic Wikipedia-style response. What are the TWO most impactful things they should add?

Explanation: Audience (who needs this info and why) and Format (bullets, comparison table, step-by-step guide) are the two highest-impact additions. Together they transform a search-engine query into a targeted, usable prompt.

2You write a prompt with all 5 RCFCE components but the output is still mediocre. What is the most likely problem?

Explanation: Having all 5 components does not guarantee quality. Each component needs to be SPECIFIC. "You are an expert" is weaker than "You are a senior tax accountant who specializes in small business deductions." Specificity within each component is what makes the framework powerful.

3You need to create a weekly team email, a client proposal, and meeting prep — all by end of day. What is the most efficient approach?

Explanation: Templates are built for exactly this situation. You already have proven structures for emails, proposals, and meeting prep. Fill in the variables, send, iterate if needed. Three tasks done in 15 minutes instead of an hour.

4AI keeps producing output that sounds robotic despite your tone instructions. You have tried "casual tone" and "conversational voice" with no improvement. What should you try next?

Explanation: Positive tone instructions ("be casual") are often too vague. Negative constraints directly target the specific patterns making it sound robotic. Banning "furthermore," "it is important to note," and "in conclusion" forces more natural language.

5You have been iterating on a prompt for 5 rounds. Each round fixes one thing but breaks another. What should you do?

Explanation: 5 rounds with no net improvement means the foundation is flawed. But the 5 rounds taught you valuable information. Write down what failed and why BEFORE starting over. Your fresh prompt should address every issue from the start.
