AI doesn't just answer. It teaches.

Remember the best teacher you ever had? The one who could explain anything in a way that just clicked? AI can be that — available 24/7, endlessly patient, and never annoyed when you ask the same question three different ways.

The key difference between searching Google and asking AI: you can have a back-and-forth. You can say "I don't understand that part" or "can you give me a real-world example?" and the explanation adapts to you in real time.

The Technique

The "explain like I'm 10" method.

When you're learning something new, start simple and go deeper:

Level 1: The basics

"Explain how a 401(k) works like I'm 10 years old."

Level 2: Go deeper

"OK, now explain employer matching and vesting schedules. Use an example with real numbers."

Level 3: Apply it

"I make $55,000 and my employer matches 4%. How much should I contribute and why?"
Research Mode

Making decisions with AI as your research partner.

AI is incredible for those big decisions where you don't even know what questions to ask:

Buying something expensive

"I'm looking at buying a used car for under $15,000. What should I look for? What questions should I ask the dealer? What are common red flags?"

Understanding a document

"Here's my lease renewal. Can you explain what each section means in plain language and flag anything I should negotiate?"

Exploring a life change

"I'm thinking about going back to school at 35. What are my options? What should I consider financially? What do I need to know about applying?"

Trust but Verify

When to double-check AI's answers.

AI is great at explaining concepts, breaking down ideas, and helping you think through decisions. It's less reliable for very specific facts — exact prices, current laws in your state, or whether a specific business is still open.

Rule of thumb: use AI to understand the landscape and know what questions to ask. Then verify specific facts (prices, deadlines, regulations) with official sources. AI gets you 80% there; you confirm the last 20%.

Research Method

A systematic approach to researching any topic with AI.

Random questions get random answers. A structured research approach gets comprehensive understanding. Here's a method you can use for any topic, from buying a house to understanding a health condition.

Step 1: The landscape question

"I want to understand [TOPIC]. Give me the big picture — what are the key concepts, the main options or approaches, and the most important things a beginner should know? Keep it high-level."

Step 2: The deep dive

"Now go deeper on [SPECIFIC PART]. Explain how it works, what the trade-offs are, and give me a real example with actual numbers or scenarios."

Step 3: The application question

"Here's my specific situation: [YOUR DETAILS]. Based on what we've discussed, what would you recommend for me and why?"

Step 4: The blind spot check

"What am I not thinking about? What questions should I be asking that I haven't? What mistakes do people commonly make with [TOPIC]?"

Learning Strategies

Advanced prompts for learning any skill faster with AI.

AI is the most patient tutor you'll ever have. Here are specific strategies for different types of learning — each with a ready-to-use prompt.

The Feynman technique with AI

The best way to know if you understand something is to explain it. Use AI to test yourself:

"I'm going to try to explain [TOPIC] to you. Tell me where my understanding is right, where I'm wrong, and what I'm missing. Here's my explanation: [YOUR EXPLANATION]."

The analogy builder

When a concept won't stick, ask AI to explain it through something you already understand:

"Explain how [COMPLEX TOPIC] works using an analogy from [SOMETHING I KNOW WELL — cooking, sports, music, building things]. Make the analogy detailed enough that I can use it to remember the concept."

The quiz generator

Testing yourself is one of the most effective learning techniques. Let AI create the test:

"I've been learning about [TOPIC]. Create a 10-question quiz to test my understanding — mix of easy, medium, and hard. Give me the questions first, then I'll answer, and you grade me."

The study plan builder

Learning a big topic? Let AI map out the path:

"I want to learn [TOPIC] well enough to [SPECIFIC GOAL]. I can spend about 30 minutes a day. Create a 4-week learning plan with specific topics for each day and recommended resources. Start with fundamentals and build up."
Fact-Checking

How to verify what AI tells you — a practical guide.

Trust but verify isn't just a catchphrase — it's a survival skill in the AI age. Here's a practical system for knowing what to check and how to check it.

Usually reliable (light verification)

General explanations of how things work, step-by-step instructions for common tasks, writing advice and communication frameworks, brainstorming and idea generation.

Check before using (moderate verification)

Specific statistics and data points, historical dates and details, recipe measurements and cooking times, legal or financial specifics for your state or country.

Always verify independently (high verification)

Medical information, current prices and availability, business hours and location details, regulatory requirements and deadlines, academic citations and quotes.

Try It

Learn something you've been curious about.

Pick a topic you've been meaning to understand — something you've Googled before but never quite grasped. Use the three-level approach:

Explain [TOPIC] to me like I have zero background in it. Start with the absolute basics, use a real-world analogy, and then tell me the three most important things I should know about it.

After the first answer, go deeper: "Now explain [specific part] with a concrete example." Keep going until you feel like you actually get it. That's the magic of conversational learning.

Research and Learning

1What is the key advantage of learning with AI compared to searching Google?

Explanation: The lesson emphasizes the conversational advantage: you can say I do not understand that part or give me a real-world example, and the explanation adapts to you in real time. Google cannot do this — it returns static pages regardless of your level of understanding.

2According to the lesson, when should you verify AI answers with external sources?

Explanation: The rule of thumb: use AI to understand the landscape and know what questions to ask, then verify specific facts with official sources. General advice and concept explanations are usually reliable. Specific current facts need confirmation.

3What makes the three-level learning approach effective?

Explanation: The three levels build on each other: basic explanation first (what is it?), then deeper detail with examples (how does it work?), then personal application with your real numbers (what should I do?). Each level builds on the last, creating genuine understanding rather than surface familiarity.

4What type of research question is AI particularly excellent for?

Explanation: AI is incredible for the big decision moments where you do not even know what questions to ask. Whether buying a car, understanding a contract, or exploring a career change — AI helps you discover the right questions, understand the landscape, and think through your options.
