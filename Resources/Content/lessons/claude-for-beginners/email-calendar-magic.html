Most people stare at a blank email for 10 minutes, write something mediocre, then rewrite it twice. Claude skips all of that. You describe what you need, Claude writes it, you tweak and send. The key is giving Claude enough context about tone, audience, and goal.

Email Templates in Action

🤝 Cold Outreach You met someone at a conference. You want to follow up without sounding salesy.
PROMPT: Write a cold outreach email to Jordan, VP of Operations at TechScale. I met them at SaaS Growth Summit. We talked about supply chain challenges for 5 min. My company does supply chain analytics. Short, warm, not salesy — I want a conversation, not a pitch. Claude writes a natural email that references the real conversation, offers value, and asks for a 20-minute call.
📋 Meeting Follow-Up Your kickoff meeting just ended. You need to send action items before everyone forgets.
PROMPT: Write a follow-up email for the website redesign kickoff. Attendees: me (PM), 2 designers, 1 dev, client Lisa from Bloom Co. We agreed: March 15 wireframe deadline, weekly check-ins Thursdays 2pm, Lisa sends brand guidelines by Friday. Professional but energetic. Claude produces a formatted email with action items table, dates, and next steps.
🛑 Pushing Back Your manager wants you to take on more work. You are at capacity. You need to say no without burning the relationship.
PROMPT: Push back on a new project from my manager. I have two major deadlines next week. I do not want to say no — I want to say yes, but here is what needs to shift. Respectful, solution-oriented, not whiny. Claude frames it as 3 options (start late, hand off existing work, push deadline), letting the manager choose.
📊 Weekly Status Update Nobody reads your updates. You need something scannable that makes you look good.
PROMPT: Write my weekly status. I am a PM. This week: shipped new onboarding flow, fixed 3 checkout bugs, started mobile research (5 interviews done). Next week: finish research, start wireframes, Q3 planning. Blocker: waiting on API docs from engineering. Make it scannable. Claude uses headers, emoji markers, and a blocker callout — people actually read it.
Pro Tip

Always specify the tone. "Professional but warm," "direct but not rude," "casual but competent." Claude nails tone when you name it. Without a tone instruction, you get generic corporate speak.

Advanced Technique

The Reply Chain Technique.

paste thread → Claude reads context → perfect reply

This is the single most useful email trick in this course. Copy an entire email thread, paste it into Claude, and ask it to draft your reply. Claude reads the context, understands the dynamics, and writes a response that fits naturally into the conversation.

Prompt — Reply Chain Technique (copy this)
Here is an email thread:

---
[Paste the entire email thread here]
---

I am [your name/role]. Draft my reply.

Goal: [what you want to achieve — e.g., push back
respectfully, agree with conditions, ask for more info]

Tone: [e.g., collaborative but firm, warm, direct]

Keep it under 150 words.
Prompt — Meeting prep in 90 seconds
I have a meeting in [X] minutes.

Attendees: [list names and roles]
Topic: [what the meeting is about]
My role: [what you are responsible for]

Give me:
1. Three smart questions to ask
2. Two potential objections to prepare for
3. One thing I should propose

Reply Chain in Action

📧 The Scenario Marcus (VP Sales) wants to cut your $85K marketing budget by 40%. Priya (you, CMO) has strong ROI numbers. There have been 3 emails back and forth. Tension is rising. Flip to see how Claude handles it.
PROMPT: I am Priya. Draft my reply to Marcus. Be collaborative, not defensive. Last quarter generated $340K pipeline from $72K spend. Propose keeping $70K (cut $15K from photography). Suggest sharing booth costs at the conference. Concise and positive. Claude drafts a reply that leads with ROI data (4.7x return), offers a specific $15K cut, proposes the booth-sharing idea, and ends with a 15-min meeting ask — all in under 150 words.
🧠 Why This Works Claude does not just reply to the last email. It reads the entire thread and understands subtext. Flip to see what Claude picks up on.
Claude reads between the lines: • Marcus is not just cutting budget — he is protecting his conference spend. Address that directly. • Priya has leverage (strong ROI numbers) but needs to be diplomatic, not combative. • The thread is escalating — Claude de-escalates with data and collaboration. • A meeting request moves the conversation off email where tone is harder to control. This alone saves 20+ minutes per difficult email.
More Templates

Email templates for every situation.

Beyond the basics, here are prompts for the email situations that trip people up the most. These are the emails where you usually stare at the screen for 10 minutes — Claude handles them in seconds.

Prompt — Delivering Bad News
Write an email to [recipient] delivering bad news:
[describe the bad news].

Context: [relationship, history, stakes]

Be honest and direct but empathetic. Include:
- The news clearly stated up front
- A brief explanation of why
- What I am doing about it / next steps
- An offer to discuss further

Tone: [compassionate but professional]
Keep it under 150 words.
Prompt — Asking for Something
Write an email to [recipient] asking for [what you
need].

Context: [why you need it, your relationship,
any deadline]

Make it easy to say yes. Be specific about what
I need and by when. Offer to make it convenient
for them. Do not be pushy — be respectful but clear.

Tone: [warm, professional]
Keep it under 120 words.
Prompt — Thank You / Appreciation
Write a thank you email to [recipient] for
[what they did].

Make it genuine, not generic. Reference the
specific thing they did and how it impacted me
or the team. End with something forward-looking.

Tone: [warm, sincere, not over the top]
Keep it under 80 words.
When to Use Templates vs Reply Chain

Templates are best when you are starting a new email from scratch. Reply Chain is best when you are responding to an existing thread. If you have a thread to paste, always use the Reply Chain Technique — it gives Claude the full context to match the conversation's tone and direction.

Level Up

Calendar & meeting prep, handled.

Claude is surprisingly good at helping you manage your time. Here are the specific prompts that make it work.

Prompt — Weekly Calendar Review (Sunday or Monday)
Here is my calendar for this week:

[Paste your calendar — meetings, deadlines, events]

Help me:
1. Identify back-to-back meetings I should try
   to reschedule
2. Find 2-3 blocks of 90+ minutes for deep work
3. Flag any conflicts or overcommitments
4. Suggest which meetings could be emails instead
Prompt — Post-Meeting Summary Email
I just finished a meeting. Here are my rough notes:

[Paste messy notes, fragments, bullet points]

Write a professional follow-up email to all
attendees with:
- 3-5 key discussion points
- All decisions made
- Action items with owners and deadlines
- Next meeting date/agenda if applicable

Tone: professional, organized, energetic.
Pro Tip

Paste your actual calendar. Copy your week from Google Calendar, paste into Claude, and say "Help me find 2 hours for deep work and identify back-to-back meetings I should reschedule." Instant scheduling assistant.

Power Move: Claude Projects

For people you email regularly (your manager, key clients, close collaborators), create a Claude Project. Add context about the relationship, their communication style, past interactions. Now every email Claude drafts for that person is perfectly tailored.

The real magic: This isn't about AI writing your emails. It's about eliminating blank-page paralysis. When Claude drafts a starting point in 5 seconds, all you do is edit — and editing is 10x faster than creating from scratch. Most people save 30-60 minutes per day once this becomes habit.
