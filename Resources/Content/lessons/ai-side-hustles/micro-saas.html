Micro-SaaS is a tiny software tool that solves one specific problem for one specific audience and charges a monthly subscription. No VC funding. No team of 50. Just you, AI coding assistants, and a credit card processor.

Examples that print money: a Chrome extension that summarizes YouTube videos ($9/month, 5,000 users = $45K/month). A Slack bot that tracks team standup responses ($12/month per team). A simple tool that converts PDFs to branded proposals ($19/month for freelancers). None of these required a CS degree to build.

The magic of micro-SaaS is recurring revenue. A freelancer earning $5K/month has to win new projects constantly. A micro-SaaS founder earning $5K/month from 200 users at $25/month just needs to keep those users happy. Revenue compounds, effort flattens.

Finding Ideas

Your best idea is hiding in someone else's complaint.

Don't brainstorm in isolation. Hunt for problems in the wild. Here are the five best hunting grounds:

1. Reddit & Indie Hackers: Search subreddits like r/smallbusiness, r/freelance, or r/SaaS for posts starting with "I wish there was..." or "Is there a tool that..." Each one is a validated pain point.

2. Upwork & Fiverr: Look for services that are repetitive and rules-based. If someone pays $50/gig for something that could be automated, you've found a micro-SaaS idea. The service price tells you the willingness to pay.

3. Existing tool reviews: Read 1-star and 2-star reviews of popular SaaS tools. "I love Calendly but I wish it could..." is your product roadmap. Build the feature they want as a standalone tool.

4. Your own frustrations: What repetitive task do you do every week that annoys you? If it annoys you, it annoys thousands of others. Scratch your own itch.

5. AI workflow gaps: People using ChatGPT or Claude for work constantly need bridges — tools that connect AI outputs to real workflows. Prompt managers, output formatters, and AI-to-spreadsheet connectors are all wide open.

Idea validation in 24 hours: Post your concept on Reddit, Indie Hackers, or Twitter. Create a simple landing page with Carrd ($19/year) describing the problem and solution. Add a "Join the waitlist" button. If 50+ people sign up in a week without ads, you have something. If not, move on.
The Build Stack

You don't need to be a developer. You need to be a director.

AI coding tools have made it possible for non-developers to build functional web apps. Here's the stack:

Cursor + Claude: An AI-powered code editor that writes full features from natural language descriptions. Describe what you want, Cursor builds it. You review, test, and guide.

Next.js + Supabase: The go-to combo for indie SaaS. Next.js for the frontend, Supabase for the database and authentication. Both have generous free tiers. Cursor can scaffold an entire app with this stack in hours.

Vercel: One-click deployment. Push your code to GitHub, Vercel deploys it automatically. Free for hobby projects, $20/month for production.

Stripe: Payment processing. Integration takes 30 minutes with AI assistance. Handles subscriptions, invoicing, and tax compliance. They take 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction.

Alternative no-code path: If coding feels too intimidating, use Bubble.io or Softr to build your MVP. Lower ceiling but faster launch. You can always rebuild with code once you've validated demand.

Realistic timeline: Weekend 1 — build core feature. Weekend 2 — add auth, payments, and polish. Weekend 3 — launch. Total investment: ~$50 in hosting/domains and 30-40 hours of focused work.

Launch & Growth

First 100 users define your entire trajectory.

Launch channels that work for micro-SaaS:

Product Hunt: Free to launch. Prepare a compelling tagline, 4-5 screenshots, and a short demo video (Loom works fine). A top-10 finish can bring 500-2,000 signups in 24 hours.

Indie Hackers / Hacker News: Share your building journey. "I built a [tool] in a weekend using AI" posts consistently hit the front page. Authenticity beats polish here.

Niche communities: Go where your users already hang out. Built a tool for teachers? Post in education Facebook groups. Built one for Shopify stores? Share in Shopify subreddits. Direct, targeted, free.

SEO content: Write 5-10 blog posts targeting "[your niche] + tool/software/app." These compound over months and become your primary traffic source by month 6.

Pricing psychology: Start with three tiers. Free (limited features, drives adoption), Pro ($9-$29/month, core features), and Team ($49-$99/month, collaboration features). The free tier is your marketing engine — most micro-SaaS tools convert 3-5% of free users to paid.
