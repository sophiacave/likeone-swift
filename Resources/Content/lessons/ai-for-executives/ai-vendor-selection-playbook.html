Red Flags

7 red flags that should end a conversation.

1. "Our AI is proprietary"

If they won't tell you what model they're built on, they're hiding something. Most enterprise AI tools use OpenAI, Anthropic, or open-source models underneath. That's fine. Lying about it isn't.

2. No data retention policy

Ask: "What happens to our data after we cancel?" If the answer is vague, walk away. Your data should be exportable, deletable, and never used for training without explicit consent.

3. ROI claims without methodology

"Our customers see 400% ROI" means nothing without knowing who, how, and over what timeframe. Demand case studies with named companies and verifiable metrics.

4. No pilot or proof-of-concept option

Any vendor confident in their product will let you test it on real data for 30-90 days. Annual contracts with no trial period are a red flag.

5. Per-seat pricing that scales exponentially

AI costs are dropping every quarter. If a vendor's pricing model gets dramatically more expensive as you scale, their economics don't work. You'll be paying for their inefficiency.

6. "We replace your team"

No responsible AI vendor makes this claim. AI augments teams. Vendors who promise to eliminate headcount are selling a fantasy and setting you up for implementation failure.

7. No SOC 2 or equivalent compliance

If they're handling your enterprise data without SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, or equivalent certification, they're not ready for enterprise customers. Full stop.

The Demo

Your AI demo checklist.

Bring this to every vendor meeting. Score each item 1-5. Any vendor scoring below 30/50 doesn't make the shortlist.

Before the demo — Send the vendor YOUR data (anonymized if needed) and ask them to demo with it. Canned demos with perfect data prove nothing. How the tool handles your messy, real-world data is the only thing that matters.
Integration

Does it connect to your existing stack? CRM, ERP, data warehouse? Native integrations beat "we have an API" every time.

Time to value

How long from contract signing to first measurable result? Anything over 90 days for a SaaS tool is a warning sign.

Accuracy on your data

Run their tool on 100 real examples. Measure accuracy yourself. Don't trust their benchmarks.

Explainability

Can you see WHY the AI made a decision? Black-box AI is unacceptable in regulated industries and risky everywhere else.

Failure handling

What happens when the AI gets it wrong? Is there a human escalation path? How does the system learn from mistakes?

Admin controls

Can you set guardrails, restrict data access, manage permissions? Enterprise AI without governance features isn't enterprise-ready.

Support model

Who helps when things break? Dedicated CSM? 24/7 support? Community forum? Match the support level to your team's technical maturity.

Roadmap transparency

Where is the product going? How do they handle model upgrades? A vendor that won't share their roadmap doesn't trust you as a partner.

Exit strategy

How easy is it to leave? Data portability, contract flexibility, migration support. The best vendors make it easy to leave because they know you won't.

Reference customers

Talk to at least two current customers in your industry. Ask what surprised them. Ask what almost made them cancel.

The Contract

Negotiate like the AI market is in your favor. Because it is.

There are more AI vendors than enterprise customers. That gives you leverage. Use it.

Five non-negotiables for every AI contract:

  • Performance SLAs — Define minimum accuracy, uptime, and response time. Tie them to credits or termination rights.
  • Data ownership clause — Your data stays yours. Period. No training on your data without written consent. Full deletion on termination.
  • Price protection — Cap annual increases at 5-8%. AI costs are dropping; your vendor shouldn't be raising prices 20% annually.
  • Quarterly exit option — Push for 90-day termination for convenience after the first year. If they won't budge, demand a 30-day termination for cause with clearly defined triggers.
  • Model change notification — If the vendor switches the underlying AI model, you get 60 days notice and the right to test before the change goes live.
The Decision

The build-vs-buy decision matrix.

This isn't about technical preference. It's about where AI creates competitive advantage for your organization versus where it's a utility.

The rule of thumb: If AI is touching your core differentiator, build. If it's optimizing a back-office process that every company has, buy. If you're unsure, buy first, learn from it, then build what matters.
Buy when...

The problem is common across industries. Time to value matters more than customization. Your team lacks ML engineering talent. The vendor has domain expertise you don't.

Build when...

The AI IS your product or core differentiator. You have proprietary data that creates a moat. You need full control over model behavior. Regulatory requirements demand it.

Partner when...

You need custom but can't hire fast enough. The problem requires domain expertise AND AI expertise. You want to build internal capability over time with expert guidance.

Wait when...

The use case is experimental with no clear ROI. The technology isn't mature enough for your requirements. Your data infrastructure isn't ready. Regulation is still evolving.

Vendor Evaluation Criteria

SOC 2 Type II
The minimum security certification required before sharing enterprise data with any AI vendor. Equivalent standards: ISO 27001, FedRAMP for government.
Pilot / Proof of Concept
A 30-90 day trial on YOUR real data. Any confident vendor offers this. No pilot = no deal.
Data Ownership Clause
A contract term stating your data stays yours, cannot be used for model training without written consent, and must be fully deleted on termination.
Performance SLA
Contractual minimums for accuracy, uptime, and response time, tied to service credits or termination rights if not met.
Model Change Notification
A contract clause giving you 60 days notice and testing rights before a vendor switches the underlying AI model powering their product.

Explanation: Any vendor who won't let you test their product on your real data before committing is not confident in their product. No pilot is a disqualifying red flag.

2Which of the following is a NON-NEGOTIABLE contract term for AI vendor agreements?

Explanation: Data ownership is one of the five non-negotiables. Your data stays yours, cannot be used for training without written consent, and must be fully deletable on termination.

3A vendor claims their AI is 'proprietary' and won't disclose what model it's built on. This is:

Explanation: Most enterprise AI tools are built on OpenAI, Anthropic, or open-source models. That is fine. Refusing to disclose the underlying model is not fine — it often means they're hiding a commodity wrapper or have something to conceal.

4When should you BUILD a custom AI solution rather than buying a vendor product?

Explanation: Build when AI touches your core differentiator or when you have proprietary data that creates a competitive moat. For common back-office problems, buying a vendor solution is faster and cheaper.
