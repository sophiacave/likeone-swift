The Structure

Every high-converting landing page follows this structure.

Landing Page: Low vs High Conversion
Low Conversion
Feature-heavy headline. No social proof. "Submit" button. Navigation links everywhere. Multiple CTAs competing.
1-2% conversion rate
High Conversion
Benefit headline under 10 words. Testimonials near CTA. "Start Free Trial" button. One page, one goal. 8th-grade reading level.
5-12% conversion rate
The 6-part structure: hero, problem, solution, proof, objections, final CTA.
1
Hero: Headline + Sub + CTA
Clear value proposition in 10 words or less. What you get + why it matters.
2
Problem: Agitate the pain
Show you understand their frustration. Make them feel seen.
3
Solution: Your product as the answer
How your product solves the problem. Features as benefits, not specs.
4
Social Proof: Evidence it works
Testimonials, logos, numbers, case studies. Real results from real people.
5
Objection Handling: Remove doubt
FAQ section, guarantee, risk-reversal. Address the reasons they might NOT buy.
6
Final CTA: Last chance to convert
Repeat the offer. Urgency if applicable. Make the button impossible to miss.
The Prompt

Generate complete landing page copy.

Write landing page copy for:

Product: [Name + what it does]
Price: [Price point or pricing model]
Target audience: [Specific buyer persona]
Main pain point: [The #1 problem you solve]
Key differentiator: [Why you, not the alternative?]
Social proof: [Testimonials, numbers, logos you have]

Write these sections:
1. Hero headline (under 10 words) + subheadline + CTA button text
2. Problem section (3-4 sentences — agitate the pain)
3. Solution section (3 benefits as feature→benefit pairs)
4. Social proof section (format the testimonials I gave you)
5. FAQ section (5 objection-handling Q&As)
6. Final CTA section (urgency + button + reassurance)

Rules:
- Every sentence should make them want to read the next one
- Benefits over features. "Save 10 hours/week" not "automated workflow engine"
- Write at an 8th-grade reading level
- CTA buttons: action verbs, not "Submit" or "Learn More"
Headlines

The headline makes or breaks the page.

Generate 20 headline options and pick the one that stops you in your tracks:

Social proof: "10,000 Marketers Switched. Here's Why."
Direct: "The Marketing Tool That Pays For Itself in Week One"
Pain: "Stop Wasting Money on Marketing That Doesn't Work"

Ask AI for "20 landing page headlines in 5 different styles: benefit, question, social proof, direct offer, and pain-agitate." Then pick your favorite from each style and test them.

CRO Patterns

7 conversion rate optimization patterns that work.

Beyond the page structure, these CRO patterns consistently lift conversion rates. Ask AI to incorporate them into your landing page copy:

1
Directional cues
Arrows, eye-line from photos, and visual flow that guide the reader's eye toward the CTA. Ask AI to suggest where to place visual cues in your layout.
2
Anchoring
Show a higher price first, then reveal the actual price. "Agencies charge $5,000/month. Get the same results for $99." The contrast makes the real price feel small.
3
Risk reversal
"30-day money-back guarantee. No questions asked." Remove every reason someone might hesitate. The easier it is to undo the purchase, the easier it is to make it.
4
Specificity in numbers
"2,847 businesses use this" converts better than "Thousands of businesses use this." Specific numbers feel real. Round numbers feel made up.
5
Microcopy near CTAs
Small text near the button that reduces anxiety: "No credit card required," "Cancel anytime," "Takes 30 seconds." These tiny phrases remove friction at the moment of decision.
6
Social proof placement
Place testimonials near the CTA, not just in a dedicated section. A quote right above the "Buy Now" button reinforces confidence at the moment of action.
7
One page, one goal
Every landing page should have exactly one desired action. No navigation menu, no sidebar links, no "also check out" sections. Every element serves the single conversion goal.
Testing

What to A/B test on your landing page — in priority order.

Not all tests are equal. Test these elements in order of impact — starting with the changes that move the needle most:

Highest impact: The headline. It's the first thing visitors read and the #1 factor in whether they stay or bounce. Ask AI for 20 headline variations and test the top 2.
High impact: The CTA button text and color. "Start Free Trial" vs "See It In Action" can produce a 20%+ difference in click rates.
Medium impact: Social proof format. Test a single powerful testimonial vs multiple short quotes vs a customer logo bar. Different audiences respond to different proof formats.
Medium impact: Page length. Test a short page (hero + CTA + social proof) vs a long page (full 6-section structure). Short pages often win for simple offers; long pages win for high-ticket products.
Lower impact: Microcopy, image choices, and layout details. These matter, but test them after you've optimized the big elements. A great headline with mediocre images beats mediocre headlines with great images.

Knowledge Check

Check your understanding.

Landing Pages and Conversion

1How long should a landing page hero headline be?

Explanation: The lesson specifies the hero headline should be under 10 words. The value proposition must be immediately clear — what you get and why it matters, distilled to its essence. Shorter is almost always more powerful.

2What reading level should landing page copy be written at?

Explanation: The lesson specifies 8th-grade reading level. Clear, accessible copy converts better than impressive-sounding jargon. Complexity creates friction; simplicity creates conversions.

3What is wrong with CTA buttons that say Submit or Learn More?

Explanation: The lesson's rule: CTA buttons should use action verbs, not Submit or Learn More. Examples of strong CTAs describe the specific outcome: Start Free Trial, Get Instant Access, See It In Action. The button text should make the benefit of clicking obvious.

4What is the purpose of a final CTA section at the bottom of a landing page?

Explanation: The final CTA section gives one last chance to convert readers who made it to the bottom. It repeats the offer, reinforces urgency if genuine, and makes the button impossible to miss. Not everyone converts at the top — the final CTA catches those who needed more convincing.
