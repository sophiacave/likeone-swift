Master five ways your brand makes money. step by step.

A personal brand without monetization is a hobby. Nothing wrong with hobbies -- but you're here to build something that pays. Here are the five revenue streams, ranked by effort to launch:

1. Freelance & Consulting: The fastest path to revenue. Your brand attracts clients who already trust you. Inbound consulting leads close 3-5x faster than cold outreach because your content pre-sold them on your expertise.
2. Digital Products: Courses, templates, guides, prompts, toolkits. Create once, sell forever. AI lets you build these in days, not months. A $29 template that sells 100 copies is $2,900 while you sleep.
3. Paid Community: A Discord, Slack, or Circle community where members pay monthly for access to you and each other. Recurring revenue with compounding value. Start at $19-49/month.
4. Speaking & Workshops: Paid talks, corporate workshops, bootcamps. Rates range from $500 for local events to $10,000+ for keynotes. Your content is your audition tape.
5. Sponsorships & Partnerships: Once your audience is large enough, brands pay to reach them. Newsletter sponsors, social media partnerships, affiliate deals. Typically kicks in around 5,000+ engaged followers.
Pricing

Stop undercharging. Here's how to price right.

Most people price based on what they'd pay, not what the market will pay. That's how you end up charging $50/hour when you should be charging $250.

Value-based pricing: Don't charge for your time. Charge for the outcome. If your consulting saves a company $100,000/year, charging $10,000 for that engagement is a bargain. Frame your price around the value delivered, not the hours spent.

The pricing ladder: Free content builds trust. A $29 digital product proves they'll pay. A $299 course proves they'll invest. A $2,999 consulting package proves they'll commit. Each rung validates the next. Never skip rungs -- build the ladder.

AI for pricing research: "What do [your niche] consultants typically charge for [service]? Give me a range from entry-level to premium, and explain what justifies the premium pricing." Use this as a baseline, then position yourself based on your unique value.

Digital Products

Build a product this week with AI.

Your first digital product doesn't need to be a 40-hour course. Start with something small that solves a specific problem:

Templates and frameworks: Notion templates, spreadsheet frameworks, prompt libraries, checklists. AI helps you create them. Canva helps you make them look professional. Gumroad or Lemonsqueezy handles the selling. Total build time: 1-2 days.

Mini-courses: A 5-lesson email course or a 60-minute video workshop. Not a massive production. Just you sharing exactly how you do something, step by step. Price: $29-99.

"I'm an expert in [your niche]. My audience struggles with [specific problem]. Create an outline for a digital product that solves this. Include: product name, description, what's included (templates, guides, videos), pricing suggestion, and a landing page headline."

Validation before building: Before you spend a week building something, test demand. Post about the idea on social media. Create a waitlist landing page. If 50 people sign up, build it. If 5 people sign up, try a different angle. AI makes pivoting cheap.

Launch Playbook

The 7-day launch that actually works.

You don't need a 6-week launch campaign. Here's the 7-day playbook for your first product:

Day 1-2: Build the product. Use AI to draft content, Canva for design, your platform of choice for delivery.

Day 3: Create the sales page. One clear headline, three bullet points on what they get, social proof if you have it, a price, and a buy button. That's it.

Day 4: Soft launch to your email list. "I made this for you. Here's why." Your warmest audience buys first and gives you testimonials.

Day 5-6: Social media launch. Share the story behind why you built it. Show behind-the-scenes. Post testimonials from early buyers.

Day 7: Final push. "Last chance" or "early pricing ends tonight." Urgency works when it's honest.

Your first launch will feel small. Maybe you sell 10 copies. That's a win. You now have proof of concept, testimonials, and a product that sells passively forever. The second launch will be bigger. The tenth will be transformative.
